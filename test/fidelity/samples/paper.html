Adaptive Sampling for Low-Latency Telemetry Aggregation in Edge Networks

Amira Hassan1, Tomás Ferreira2, and Lin Wei Chen1,2
1Department of Computer Science, University of Malaya, Kuala Lumpur · 2Instituto Superior Técnico, Lisboa
Abstract—We present a sampling scheme that adapts its rate to the local variance of an incoming telemetry stream, keeping aggregate error under 2% while cutting uplink traffic by 64% on a trace of 1.3 billion events. The method requires O(1) state per stream and runs on commodity gateways. We analyse its bias, compare it with fixed-rate and reservoir baselines, and describe a deployment across 214 edge sites.

I. Introduction

Edge deployments generate telemetry faster than constrained uplinks can carry it. Uniform down-sampling is simple but wastes bandwidth on quiet streams while under-sampling bursts[1]. We instead let each gateway estimate local variance σ2 over a sliding window and select a rate r accordingly.

Our contributions are threefold: a rate controller with provable bounds, an unbiased estimator that corrects for the varying rate, and an open evaluation on production traces.

II. Method

A. Rate controller

Given window variance σ2t and a target error ε, the controller sets

rt = min(1, max(rmin, κ · σt / ε))(1)

where κ is a calibration constant. The estimator weights each retained sample by 1/rt, which makes the aggregate unbiased.

B. Complexity

Each stream keeps a running mean, a running variance and one counter, so state is O(1). Per-event cost is a handful of arithmetic operations and one random draw.

III. Evaluation

We compare against uniform sampling and reservoir sampling on three traces (Table I). Our method reaches the target error with the least traffic in every case.

IV. Conclusion

Variance-driven sampling gives a large traffic reduction at a small, controllable error. Code and traces are available at example.org/adaptive-sampling.

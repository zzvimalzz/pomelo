# Image editor

A window that shows pictures, one tab for each. It opens from the Edit wheel (drag a picture, tap Shift, drop it on **Edit**), draws the picture with WebGL2 and sends nothing over the network. A picture can be turned, flipped, cropped, levelled and adjusted for light and colour, with a curve, a colour mixer and colour grading, the changes can be undone, and it can be saved over its file or as a copy.

## How it works

1. `src/app/main.js` creates the editor with `createEditor(deps)` and calls `registerIpc()` at start-up. `main.js` requires only `index.js`; `editor.window.js` is loaded the first time a picture is opened.
2. `src/app/drop-flow.js` offers the Edit wheel when every dragged file is a picture (`canEdit`, which is `limits.js`'s `editable` applied to `extOf`). A drop on it calls `open(paths)`.
3. `open(paths)` is served one call at a time. For each path `images.js` checks that it is a file, decodes it once with `readImage` (the conversion feature's, in `engines/builtin/images.js`: upright through the EXIF orientation, converted to sRGB, RGBA, 8 bits) and keeps it under a random 32-byte token. A picture that is open already (the same path, whatever its case) is shown again. At most ten are open (`Too many images open`, `Close one to open another.`). A picture that is refused gets one notice for the whole call: `This image is too large to edit` (over 50 megapixels or 16 384 pixels on a side), `Can't edit an animated image`, or `Can't open this image` with the reason. The page is never given a path.
4. The window opens on the first picture, or, when one is open, receives the new pictures with `imged:opened` and comes to the front. Closing the last tab closes the window and the main process forgets every picture.
5. The page asks `imged:state` for the open pictures, the remembered view and the piece size, then reads each picture's pixels in pieces of 16 MiB with `imged:pixels(token, begin, end)`. Each piece is a copy, because Electron would send the whole of a typed array's buffer for a view of it. When a picture is read the page calls `imged:pixels-done` and the main process frees its copy. The page keeps the pixels with the tab, because it must be able to make the texture again.
6. `ui/render.js` makes a WebGL2 context that does no premultiplying and no colour conversion, uploads a picture as an `RGBA8` texture and builds its mipmaps once. One fragment shader (`ui/shaders.js`) maps each canvas pixel to a picture pixel from the zoom and the picture's place, so a frame costs the pixels on screen whatever the picture's size. Linear filtering is used from 50 % zoom up, mipmaps below it. The texture exists only while its tab is on show.
7. `ui/view.js` holds the zoom and the place of the picture. The canvas is in device pixels, so 100 % is one picture pixel for each screen pixel. Fit leaves 32 px of air and never enlarges a picture past 100 %, the largest zoom is 800 %, and a picture can be pushed until its edge is 32 px inside the window. The buttons, the keys and the double click glide to a zoom in 220 ms (at once with reduced motion); Ctrl and the mouse wheel follow the hand. The zoom value opens a menu of Fit, 25 %, 50 %, 100 %, 200 %, 400 % and 800 %: the one in use is ticked, a zoom below Fit is greyed out, and Up, Down and Esc work in it.
8. The chequerboard is an element behind the canvas, placed over the picture's rectangle and shown only when the picture can be transparent (`hasAlpha` from sharp's metadata). Its two tones are `--check-a` and `--check-b` in `ui/editor.css`.
9. A picture that is larger than the graphics card's texture limit, a page with no WebGL2 and a lost context all show the card **Can't show this image** (`Editing needs graphics acceleration, which is off on this PC.`) in place of the picture.
10. An edit is a plain object, `{ exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, temperature, tint, vibrance, saturation, grading, curve, mixer, geometry: { rotate, flipH, flipV, angle, aspect, portrait, crop } }`, and a tab keeps its history of them (`ui/model.js`): a list of snapshots, at most 100. Undo steps through results, not moves. A change equal to the one on show does nothing; a change drops what could be redone; arriving back at an earlier snapshot (four quarter turns, two flips of one axis) drops the steps after it; a change made by the same control as the last step replaces that step; anything else is a new step. A tab has changes while its snapshot differs from the one last saved, and the page tells the main process with `imged:dirty`, which is how the window knows to hold its closing.
11. `ui/geometry.js` turns the geometry into the size of the shown picture and a 3 x 3 matrix. The shader (`ui/shaders.js`) finds, for each pixel of the canvas, the point of the shown picture and then, through the matrix, the point of the source. The picture is turned first and flipped second, so a flip stays on the same side of the screen, and with one flip on a quarter turn to the right is stored as a turn to the left. Then it is levelled by `angle` degrees (positive turns it clockwise) about its centre, and cut by `crop`, a frame kept as fractions of the turned picture. Inside the shown picture a point may stray one source pixel past the source's edge, so rounding leaves no clear rim on a levelled one. Outside the shown picture nothing is drawn, except in the Crop view, where whatever is still on the source is drawn darkened (`uVeil`). A turn carries the frame with it (and swaps a set shape between portrait and landscape), a flip mirrors the frame and negates the level, and frames are kept to a millionth so that two flips give back exactly what they started with. A turn glides the canvas from where the picture was in 320 ms and a flip in 300 ms (not with reduced motion); the view of a zoomed picture is turned or mirrored with it. **Compare** (hold the button or `\`) draws the source with no geometry and cross-fades for 120 ms.
12. Save renders the same shader into an `OffscreenCanvas` the size of the result, with the rows counted from the bottom (`uFlip`), and `readPixels` gives raw RGBA, so the file and the screen cannot differ. `imged:save` sends those bytes with `{ mode, format, quality, longEdge, width, height }`. `save-options.js` checks the options and `save.js` checks that the byte count is `width * height * 4`, then asks `writeImage` (the conversion feature's) to encode into a temp file and `replaceFile` to put it in place. A save over the file needs the format to be the file's own and keeps what the file looked like when it was read: if another program changed it the answer is `{ stop: 'changed' }` and the page offers **Save a copy**. A copy asks for a path in the Save dialog; a name with the wrong extension gets the right one added, and a path that is the original itself counts as a save over it. Once a copy is written the main process opens it as a tab and answers `{ ok: true, copy: 'opened' }`, and the page closes the edited tab without asking, because its changes are in the copy. A copy in a format the editor does not open (GIF, PDF) answers `copy: 'written'`: the edited tab stays as it was. A stop that needs saying (`locked`, `read-only`, `no-space`) is said once in the corner of the screen. A copy can be JPG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, HEIC, TIFF, BMP, GIF or PDF: `writeImage` (the conversion feature's) writes all of them, HEIC through the converter's Python helper, which takes a PNG file, so without that engine the answer is `{ stop: 'engine' }` and a sentence about the components to install. HEIC and SVG pictures cannot be written over, so they go straight to Save a copy, with JPG or PNG chosen. JPG, PNG, WebP and AVIF keep the original's EXIF tags with the orientation set to upright; `writeImage` does it by putting the pixels over an 8 x 8 corner of the original. The first Save over a file asks **Replace the original?** once for that tab. Closing a tab, or the window, with changes asks **Save changes to the file?** for each such tab.
13. Crop is entered from the **Crop** button in the pill, or with **C**, which swaps the pill for the crop dock (`#cropdock`, built in `ui/editor.js` and `ui/crop.js`) and puts **Reset** in the top left corner of the well and **Cancel** and **Done** in the top right. The dock has the Straighten ruler (`ui/sliders.js` with `ticks`: ±45° in tenths, a mark every degree and a longer one every five, the value above it, the same keys and typed values as any slider) and, under it, rotate left and right, the aspect button and flip across and down. The aspect button swaps the ruler for a strip of shapes (Free, Original, Square and each of 9:16, 16:9, 4:5, 5:4, 3:4, 4:3, 2:3, 3:2, 5:7, 7:5, 1:2, 2:1); choosing one closes the strip. The edit keeps the wide form of the shape with `portrait` set for the tall one. While Crop is on, the panel is dimmed and off. The crop view keeps 100 px at the bottom of the well for the dock (`CROP_DOCK` in `ui/view.js`). The Crop tool (`ui/crop.js`) keeps the crop frame (`#frame`, inside the stage so it turns with it) fitted in the middle of the well with 48 px of air, enlarging a small frame up to 800 %, and the rest of the picture is drawn darkened round it. A drag anywhere but a handle moves the picture under the frame (`moveFrame` in `ui/geometry.js`), and a tilted edge lets it slide along. Ctrl and the mouse wheel (or a pinch on a touchpad) scale the frame about the point under the pointer (`zoomFrame`), which shows as the picture growing or shrinking around that point under a frame that stays; it stops at the whole picture one way, and at 800 % or a 40 pixel side the other. Each turn eases in 160 ms from where the last had got to. While a handle is dragged the view is held (`holdView` in `ui/view.js`), so the handle follows the pointer; it zooms out only as far as it must to keep a growing frame in sight, and on release glides to the new frame (`releaseView`). Undo and Redo glide the same way, and so does the view between the cropped picture and the Crop view (`setCropping`). Each glide takes 450 ms, starts and ends at rest, and moves the frame in a straight line across the screen as it zooms (`ease` and `mix`). All of them happen at once with reduced motion, and the pill, zoom, pan and Compare are off while Crop is on. A drag of a handle or the picture, or a change of the Straighten slider, changes the tab's edit with `live`, which is not in the history. The change is committed when the drag ends, which a key, a tab switch or a save does early, and consecutive ones of one visit to the tool fold into one step. Choosing an aspect eases the frame to its new size in the same time and then commits. The frame is kept inside the levelled picture (`limit`, `shrinkInside` in `ui/geometry.js`). **Original** is the shape of the turned picture, and Free and Original have no tall form. Straighten shrinks the frame from where it was when the change began, so moving the ruler back within one change gives it back. **Enter**, **Done** or another tool leaves with the crop kept (and the aspect forgotten when the whole picture is kept and not levelled, so choosing one alone is not a change); **Esc** or **Cancel** commits what Crop was entered with, which the history reads as going back to that step.

14. The Adjust tool (`ui/adjust.js`) is a histogram and five sections, the first two of sliders, **Light** (Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites, Blacks) and **Colour** (Temperature, Tint, Vibrance, Saturation). Each value is a key of the edit, 0 when neutral (`RANGES` in `ui/model.js` holds the limits: Exposure -4 to 4 by hundredths, the rest -100 to 100 by ones). The shader applies them to each source pixel before the crop veil, in this order: white balance and exposure as a gain on the linear light (the white balance is scaled to keep the luminance, so Temperature and Tint change colour and not brightness), Highlights and Shadows as a lift weighted by luminance, the black and white points from Blacks and Whites, Contrast as an S curve about the middle, then Vibrance and Saturation in HSL. A picture with every value at 0 skips all of it (`uOn`), so its pixels pass through exactly. `ui/render.js` turns an edit into the shader's uniforms; Compare draws with a neutral edit, and Save draws the tab's own. A slider (`ui/sliders.js`) changes the tab's edit with `live` while it moves, which is not in the history, and commits one step when the change ends: when the pointer lets go, 400 ms after the last key, or when a typed value is accepted. Steps from one control fold into one (the key is the control's label), so a drag, or a drag that goes back to 0, leaves one step or none. A key, a tab switch or a save ends a pending change early (`settle`). Left and Right change a slider by one step, Shift by ten, Page Up and Page Down by ten, Home sets neutral, a double click on the row does too, and a click on the value types one. The thumb stays 9 px inside the track, so its ring and its growth on hover are not cut off at either end, and a slider with the focus shows no ring. The histogram (`ui/histogram.js`) is drawn from a 256 pixel copy of the picture (`sample` in `ui/render.js`, drawn off screen by the same shader) of the picture as it is edited, 80 ms after the last change. A section's reset arrow shows only while it differs from neutral. Which sections are open is kept in `imageView.sections`.

15. The other three sections are tools that `adjust.js` builds (`build` in its `SECTIONS`) and hands a `slider` function, so their sliders are kept up to date and settled like the others. Their parts of the edit are `grading`, `curve` and `mixer`, and the section's reset arrow puts the parts it owns back to what `neutral()` holds. **Colour grading** (`ui/grading.js`) has three 76 px wheels, **Shadows**, **Midtones** and **Highlights**, with the **Balance** slider under them: the angle of the puck is the hue (0 to 360, red to the right) and its distance from the middle is the amount (0 to 100), a drag is one step (`grading-<region>`) and a double click puts the wheel back. The shader adds to each pixel the colour of the hue with its own brightness taken out, times the amount and a quarter, weighted by how shadowy, midtone or highlight the pixel is; Balance moves the edge between the shadows and the highlights from the middle (`uShade`, `uBalance`). **Curve** (`ui/curve-editor.js`) has the chips **RGB**, **Red**, **Green** and **Blue** and a 288 x 220 box that draws all four curves, the chosen one on top and thicker with its points, over a faint shape of the picture's luminance (read from the copy the histogram uses). A click adds a point on the line (up to eight, at least 1 % of the width from its neighbours) and a drag moves it; the two ends move up and down only; dragging an inner point more than 12 % out of the box dims it and lets go of it, and so does a double click. A drag is one step (`curve-<channel>`). `ui/curves.js` draws a monotone cubic through the points (a curve through points that rise never dips) and `lut` turns the four curves into a 256 x 1 texture: the RGB curve first, then each channel's own. `ui/render.js` makes that texture again only when a curve changed. **Colour mixer** (`ui/mixer.js`) has eight colours (red, orange, yellow, green, aqua, blue, purple and magenta, at 0, 30, 60, 120, 180, 240, 270 and 300 degrees of hue) and Hue, Saturation and Luminance sliders for the one chosen, each -100 to 100; the Hue track and the other two tracks take the colour chosen. In the shader a pixel's hue lies between two neighbouring colours and takes a share of each move by its distance from them, scaled down for pixels with little saturation so greys stay grey: Hue turns it by up to 30 degrees, Saturation scales its saturation, Luminance moves its lightness by up to 0.3 of its saturation. The order in the shader is: the adjustments of Light, the curves, the mixer, Vibrance and Saturation, then the grading. `adjusted` in `ui/model.js` says whether an edit has anything but its geometry to apply, which is what `uOn` is.

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `index.js` | `createEditor`: opening, the queue, notices, the window, the remembered view. The only file the app requires |
| `images.js` | The open pictures, their tokens, pieces of pixels. No Electron |
| `limits.js` | Ten tabs, 50 megapixels, 16 384 pixels a side, 16 MiB pieces, the formats that can be edited and the ones that can be written |
| `save.js` | Checks the pixels the page sends, encodes them and puts the file in place. No Electron |
| `save-options.js` | `check`: a clean copy of the save options, or an error. No Electron |
| `view.js` | `cleanView`: the valid fields of `imageView`. No Electron |
| `editor.ipc.js` | The `imged:*` handlers; each refuses a sender that is not the editor's page |
| `editor.window.js` | The window, made with `createAppWindow` (`src/shared/electron/windows.js`) |
| `ui/editor.html`, `editor.css`, `editor.js` | The page, its look, the tabs, the tool switch, the edits and saving |
| `ui/view.js`, `render.js`, `shaders.js`, `keys.js` | Zoom and pan, Compare, WebGL2 and export, the shader, every shortcut in one table |
| `ui/geometry.js`, `model.js` | Turn, flip, level and crop maths and the matrix, the edit and its history |
| `ui/adjust.js`, `sliders.js`, `histogram.js` | The Adjust tool: the sections, the slider row every tool shares, the histogram |
| `ui/grading.js`, `curve-editor.js`, `mixer.js`, `curves.js` | The Colour grading wheels, the Curve box, the Colour mixer, and the curve maths and table |
| `ui/crop.js` | The Crop tool: the frame and its handles, dragging the picture under it, the aspect strip and the Straighten ruler |
| `ui/dialogs.js` | The cards: Replace the original, Save changes, Save a copy |
| `ui/preload.js` | `window.editorApi`; reads a drop and sends its paths on |

## Contracts

- IPC: `imged:state`, `imged:pixels`, `imged:pixels-done`, `imged:close`, `imged:drop`, `imged:show`, `imged:view`, `imged:full-screen`, `imged:save`, `imged:dirty` and `imged:close-window` go from the page; `imged:opened`, `imged:closing` and `imged:full-screen-changed` go to it. `test/unit/app/ipc.test.js` pairs them with their handlers.
- `settings.json` key `imageView`: `{ window, sections, panel }`. `panel` is whether the right panel is open; `sections` is the list of open panel sections, from `light`, `colour`, `grading`, `curve`, `mixer`, `effects` and `detail`. The page can set only `sections` and `panel`, and `cleanView` drops any field that is not valid.
- The page loads under a strict CSP (`script-src 'self'`, no inline style) in its own session (partition `pomelo-editor`), sandboxed, with every http, https, ws and ftp request cancelled.

## Shortcuts

**Ctrl+Z** (Undo), **Ctrl+Y** or **Ctrl+Shift+Z** (Redo), **Ctrl+S** (Save), **Ctrl+Shift+S** (Save a copy), **R** and **Shift+R** (turn right and left), **\\** (hold to Compare), **Ctrl+=**, **Ctrl+-**, **Ctrl+0** (Fit), **Ctrl+1** (100 %), **Ctrl+B** (panel), **F11** (full screen), **Esc** (closes a card, the More menu, the zoom menu or the aspect strip, then puts back what Crop was entered with, then leaves full screen), **Enter** (in Crop, keeps the crop), **A** and **P** (the Adjust and Presets tools of the panel) and **C** (Crop). Single keys do nothing while a card is up or a field has the focus.

## Testing

`node test/run.js --only=editor-images,editor-limits,editor-view,editor-save-options,editor-save,editor-geometry,editor-model,editor-curves,drop-flow,ipc` runs the unit tests in a few seconds. `editor-save` writes real files with sharp. `npx electron test/ui/editor-shot.js` opens the real window on pictures it makes: a rotated photo, a transparent PNG, a GIF that is not offered the editor, refused pictures, ten tabs, zoom, the zoom menu, the panel, full screen, the notice for lost graphics, then turning, flipping, undo, Compare, Save a copy, Save over a file, closing with changes, a read-only file, a check that the screen and the saved file agree, and the Crop tool: a square crop, Esc and Enter, dragging a handle and the picture under the frame with the mouse, zooming with Ctrl and the wheel, the glides with Windows animations on and off, and a levelled picture drawn whole and saved with no transparent edge; then the Adjust panel: the sliders with their keys, typing and double click, one history step for a drag, the section reset arrows, the remembered sections, a mid grey brightened by Exposure and checked against the formula on screen and in the saved file, and Saturation at -100 saved with equal red, green and blue; then the Curve box (adding, dragging, removing and undoing a point, the Red curve alone, the saved file against the screen), the Colour mixer (a pure red turned by 30 degrees, one colour leaving another alone) and the Colour grading wheels (a red shadows wheel tinting only the dark half of a picture, Balance, a double click). It saves light and dark screenshots in `test/shots` and opens windows, so run it alone.

## Known limits

- The edits are turning, flipping, cropping and levelling in the crop dock, and the Light, Colour, Colour grading, Curve and Colour mixer sections of Adjust. Adjust has no other sections and the Presets tool has no controls.
- The Curve box and the grading wheels are for the mouse and a touchpad: they have no keys.
- Straighten is a ruler with the keys of any slider: dragged, typed or moved with the keys, and a double click sets it back to 0°. In Crop the view follows the frame: the zoom buttons, keys and double click are off, and Ctrl and the mouse wheel change the crop itself.
- Save sends the whole picture to the main process in one message (200 MB at 50 megapixels) and needs free space of that size plus 64 MB on the target drive. A JPG is written with plain libjpeg, about 15 % larger than the converter's.
- GIF and BMP are written flat (a GIF has no partial transparency, a BMP none at all), and a TIFF, GIF, BMP, PDF or HEIC carries no EXIF.
- A GIF is not opened in the editor, whether or not it is animated; it can still be a format of a copy.
- Animated pictures are refused, and so is anything over 50 megapixels or 16 384 pixels on a side.
- Each open tab keeps its decoded pixels in the page (4 bytes a pixel), so ten large pictures take a few hundred megabytes.
- The glass window of Windows 11 cannot be captured by the screenshot test; the opaque window is what it checks.

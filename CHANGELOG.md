# Changelog

All notable changes to Pomelo are listed here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/) and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

Every entry starts with a bold headline, then the detail. The release history in Pomelo's Settings is read from this file: it shows the headlines and leaves out the **Internal** section, so write the headline for a user and keep the detail for developers.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Edit images from the wheel.** Drag a picture, press **Shift** and drop it on **Edit** to open it in a new editor window. Pictures open as tabs (ten at most; a picture that is open is shown again, not opened twice), shown upright, with a chequerboard behind any transparency. Zoom with the buttons, **Ctrl+=**, **Ctrl+-**, **Ctrl+0** (Fit), **Ctrl+1** (100 %), **Ctrl** and the mouse wheel, a double click, or the menu that opens from the zoom value (Fit, 25 % to 800 %); drag to move a picture that is larger than the window. Fit never enlarges a small picture past 100 %. **Ctrl+B** hides the panel, **F11** goes full screen and **Esc** comes back. The editor shows pictures and does not change them. Animated pictures and pictures over 50 megapixels are refused with a notice, and a PC without graphics acceleration is told so. A picture is decoded once in the main process and handed to the page in 16 MiB pieces; the page draws it with WebGL2, so only the pixels on screen are computed. `imageView` in `settings.json` keeps the window's place and the panel, and the `imged:*` channels are the editor's.
- **A grid of every page in the PDF window.** The **Pages** button in the bar, or **P**, replaces the reading view with a grid of every page as a 160 px picture with its number, and the same key or **Esc** goes back. Select pages with a click, **Ctrl** and a click to add or remove one, **Shift** and a click for a range, **Ctrl+A** for all, a drag across empty paper, or the arrow keys, **Home**, **End** and **Space**; a bar beside the dock says how many are selected. A double click or **Enter** goes back to reading at that page. It takes the room of the pages, not of a panel, and stays up through focus reading. Without the PDF engine the button carries a small dot and opens a card, **Editing needs the PDF engine**, with **Install** (it downloads and installs it in the card, with **Retry** after a failure) and **Not now**; once it is installed the grid opens at once. Nothing is changed yet by selecting. Thumbnails are drawn only near the view, so a 3 000-page file makes 3 000 cells and a few dozen pictures. `pdfws:engine` and `pdfws:install-engine` are the new channels, and the selected and hovered pages, the ring and the bold number were checked for contrast in both themes.
- **A Document panel in the PDF window.** The info button at the right of the title row, **Ctrl+D**, or a click on the file's name opens a panel on the right with the file's name, folder, size and date, the Title, Author, Subject and Keywords, and what the file says about itself: pages, paper size, PDF version, when and by which program it was made, whether it has a password and what it does not allow, its signatures and its fonts. The name can be changed there (a name that is taken or that Windows cannot hold is refused in words, and nothing is ever replaced), **Show** opens the file's folder, and a changed Title, Author, Subject or Keywords is a change to the document that can be undone and saved like any other. The signatures are listed as facts, who the file says signed it and when, and the panel says **Pomelo does not check signatures**. The cards about the file's contents need the PDF engine. `meta.set` is the new operation, `pdf_info.py` reports the fonts and signatures, and the panel shares the right edge with Search and Links, so opening one shuts the other. The facts are read when a tab is shown, so the cards usually arrive at once and together; a slow read shows a moving line under the heading. A typed name is kept until **Rename** or Enter renames the file. A Title or other text typed back as the file had it leaves nothing to save, so the unsaved mark and **Save** go. A changed Description is appended to the file, so the pages are not read again, and text typed in the next field while it is made stays; Esc in a field puts its text back. A long place keeps the folder that holds the file in view, and the headings, the fields' edges and **Not set** were checked for contrast in both themes.
- **Tabs in the PDF window, and Open in Pomelo in Explorer.** PDFs open as tabs of one window: another PDF opened from the tray, the wheel, Explorer or the command line joins the open window, and so does a PDF dropped on it. With two or more the title row shows a row of tabs: a click or **Left**, **Right**, **Home** and **End** switch, the cross or **Delete** closes one, and the window closes with its last tab. Ten can be open, and a file that is open already is shown again, not opened twice. A tab keeps its page, zoom and layout while another is shown, and a file that cannot be shown says so in its own tab. Right-clicking a `.pdf` in Explorer has **Open in Pomelo**, and a selection of several opens them together. The tray's **Open a PDF…** dialog takes several files and starts in the folder of the PDF opened last. A tab never gets narrower than 96 px; when ten do not fit, the row scrolls sideways (the mouse wheel scrolls it too), keeps the tab on show in view and fades on the side that has more. The title row sizes its outer columns from their buttons, so the tabs keep 12 px from them however many buttons there are.
- **Links in the PDF window.** A link to another page scrolls there, and **Alt+Left** and **Alt+Right** go back and forward through the places links and bookmarks went from. A link to a web page or a mail address asks first: a card, **Open this link?**, shows the whole address as it will be opened and has **Cancel** and **Open**; Esc closes it. The card has a copy button inside the address box that copies the address and says **Copied**, and long addresses scroll in a thin scroll bar of their own. While it is up nothing behind it can be clicked, scrolled or zoomed. Web links are blue; links to other places in the document, such as contents entries, keep the text as it is. Links to files, programs, scripts and any other kind of address do nothing, and the address is never stored in the link, so a middle click or a drag takes the window nowhere.
- **A Search and Links panel in the PDF window.** The panel on the right now has two tabs, like the Pages and Outline panel on the left, and in both panels a switch slides the tab and its view across. Searching opens it on the Search tab by itself and lists the hits, and choosing the Search tab turns the floating find bar on. The new Links tab, opened with a button in the title row, lists every web and mail address in the document, page by page, including addresses written in the text, each with a globe or envelope icon for its kind. Click a row to scroll to the link, which is lit in the yellow of a search hit; use its arrow to open it (after the usual confirmation). Select some rows with their circles (the icon becomes a circle under the pointer), or all of them with the circle above the list, and Copy puts the addresses on the clipboard, one to a line. The copy button is grey until something is selected and reads Copy All when everything is. `pdfws:copy-links` checks every address with the same rule as opening one.
- **Page colours and a PDF window that opens as you left it.** The View menu has a Page colour group: Original, Sepia, Dark and Custom colours…, which opens a card with five page colours and five text colours to pick from. The file is not changed. Pictures keep their own colours, and links are light blue on a dark page. The window also remembers the zoom, whether the pages are side by side, whether the Pages panel is open, its tab and its width. Colours go through pdf.js's `pageColors`; the page's choices are checked in the main process (`view.js`) and stored in `pdfView` of `settings.json` next to the window's place and the dock.
- **Password-protected files ask for their password.** Drop a protected PDF, Word, Excel or PowerPoint file, or a protected zip or 7z archive, on a format and the wheel folds into a small card where it stood: it says the file is password protected, shows its icon and name and has a password field with a show button. A wrong password says **Wrong Password** and the button reads **Try Again**; the right one converts the file as usual. Esc or Cancel leaves the file alone. Several protected files in one drop show a row of tiles, and **Same for all** tries the password on each of them. While the card is open, dragging another file and tapping Ctrl or Shift fades the card away and shows the wheel. The card can be dragged by any part that is not the field or a button, shows in the taskbar with Pomelo's icon, sinks behind other windows when you use them, lets clicks through around it, and closes by itself after five minutes without focus, with a toast saying which file was left alone. Files that are not protected start converting at once. A protection Pomelo cannot open (OpenDocument and RAR passwords) says so instead of failing later. Protected PDF and Office files need the PDF components: the engine list in Settings now installs the package that opens them, and an update adds it to an environment Pomelo built earlier. Passwords stay in memory, are never written or logged, and are overwritten when the conversion ends.
- **Open a PDF in Pomelo.** The tray menu has **Open a PDF…**, and starting Pomelo with `--open=<path>` shows a PDF in its own window: the file's name in the title row and its pages in a scrolling view, in light or dark. The window is resizable down to 900 x 600, remembers its size, place and whether it was maximised, and is opaque before Windows 11, where there is no acrylic. Pages are read from the file in 64 KiB pieces as they are needed, so opening a 41 MB, 3 000-page file reads 1.5 MB to show the first page, nothing while it sits idle, and under 5 % of the file after scrolling 50 pages. The window cannot reach the network, and a file that is not a PDF is reported in it. The scroll bar stays out of sight until the pages scroll or the pointer is over them. Nothing of it loads until a PDF is opened.
- **Password-protected PDFs open.** The window asks for the password in a card over the document area, with an eye button to show what is typed. The card stays up while the password is checked: a wrong one turns the field red, says **Wrong Password**, keeps what was typed selected so a typo can be fixed, and the button reads **Try Again**. After five wrong ones, or on Cancel, the window says **This file is password-protected** and an **Unlock** button asks again. The password goes to pdf.js and, once pdf.js has accepted it, to Pomelo's main process, which keeps it in memory only and overwrites it when the window closes. It is never written to a file or logged.
- **Comments in a PDF show beside the page.** A note, or a highlight, underline, strike-through, drawing or shape with a comment, is a small marker beside the page, level with what it is about; pdf.js's own note icons and popups are not shown, so the page itself stays clean. Click a marker to read the comment in a card (who wrote it and when, if the file says) and to light up the text, spot or annotation it belongs to; click it again, click elsewhere or press Esc to close it. Markers never overlap, the left page keeps them on its left in two-page view, and with no room beside the page they move just inside its edge. Comments are read only.
- **Tap Shift to open a PDF from the wheel.** Drag a PDF from Explorer or the desktop and tap **Shift**: a wheel with one target, **Open**, fills the ring, and dropping the PDF on it opens it in Pomelo's PDF window. While a wheel shows, tapping the other key turns it into the other one (**Ctrl** for the conversion wheel, **Shift** for Open) in under half a second, and the new wheel takes drops from the first frame. A small pill above the wheel names the key of the other wheel. Shift only works on PDFs: with any other file, or a mix, the conversion wheel shows as usual and has no pill. With reduced motion in Windows the wheels cross-fade. The conversion wheel is drawn exactly as before, apart from the pill.
- **Page and zoom controls in the PDF window.** A bar floating at the bottom of the pages has Previous and Next, a page box (type a number and press Enter), and Zoom out, the zoom value and Zoom in. The zoom value reads **Fit page**, **Fit width** or a percentage and opens the View menu: **Fit width** (Ctrl+0), **Fit page**, **Actual size** (Ctrl+1), and a layout of **Continuous** or **Two pages side by side**. Ctrl+= and Ctrl+minus zoom too, and Ctrl with the mouse wheel zooms round the pointer wherever it is in the window, keeping the spot under it still, and no quick turn of the wheel is lost. A fit keeps fitting as the window is resized; a zoom you chose stays. The bar stays put while you scroll; a round button beside it slides it off the edge, then moves to the middle of where the bar was so one press brings it back. The bar fits inside the smallest window.
- **Focus reading for PDFs.** The button at the right of the title row, or F11, puts the PDF window full screen with only the pages showing, at Fit page. The title row returns when the pointer is at the top edge and the bar at the bottom edge, and both go when it leaves; Tab brings the bar back while the focus is in it. Page Down and the right or down arrow turn to the next page, Page Up and the left or up arrow to the previous, and a small pill shows the page number. Esc leaves, after closing an open comment or the View menu first, and the zoom you had comes back. The window's saved size and place are not changed by going full screen.
- **Results list, hit marks and a clearer current hit when finding in a PDF.** The hits are listed under a heading for each page, with the whole words round them, in the Search tab of a panel on the right, and a click goes to that hit; the Pages panel is not touched. A mark for every hit stands beside the scroll bar. The hit you are on is yellow against the pink of the others. Text can also be selected by pressing on empty paper, before a line, after it or below the last one, and dragging.
- **Find text in the PDF window.** A **Find** button beside the panel button, or Ctrl+F, opens a bar at the top with the count (`2 of 14`), **Aa** for Match case, **W** for Whole words, Previous and Next (Shift+Enter and Enter). Every hit on the page is marked, the current one stronger, and Enter goes on to the next hit on any page. Phrases that run over a line break are found, and text can be selected and copied in reading order. pdf.js's own find controller does the matching; Esc closes the bar first, then focus reading.
- **Bookmarks in the PDF window.** The panel that shows page thumbnails now has a switch at its top, **Pages** and **Outline**. Outline lists the document's bookmarks as a tree: a bookmark with others under it has a chevron, a click on a bookmark goes to its page, and the arrow keys, Enter and Space work the tree from the keyboard. Bookmarks the file marks as closed start closed, and a document without any says **This document has no bookmarks**. Nothing is read until the Outline tab is first shown, and the panel opens on the tab you last used.
- **Move the PDF window's controls to any edge.** A dotted grip at the start of the bar at the bottom of the pages can be held and dragged: four dashed places are marked (bottom, top, left and right middle) and the bar eases into the nearest when it is let go. At the left and right it stands as a column, its page arrows point up and down, and its View menu opens beside it. The place is remembered until you move it again, and with the grip focused the arrow keys move it too. In focus reading the bar returns at the edge it stands at.
- **Page thumbnails in the PDF window.** The button at the left of the title row opens a panel beside the pages with a small picture of every page; the page you are on is outlined, the panel follows as you read, and a click on a picture goes to that page. A grip on the panel's right edge sets its width, from the size it opens at up to a limit, and the pictures grow with it. It slides shut again and gives the room back, and in focus reading it floats over the left edge of the pages, opened and shut from the title row's button when the top edge brings it back, without moving the pages. Thumbnails are drawn only for the pages in view, one at a time so reading stays smooth, and the last 300 are kept, so a 3 000-page file does not hold 3 000 pictures. Nothing is built until the panel is first opened.
- **Release history in Updates.** Settings > Updates lists every release, newest first, as a folding card with its date; the version you are running is marked **Installed** and the search box finds entries inside releases. It shows the headline of each entry and nothing else, so this file keeps the detail and the history stays short. `CHANGELOG.md` now ships with the app and `src/features/updates/changelog.js` parses it when the page opens, so a new version brings its own history and nothing is edited per release. An entry without a bold headline is shown as its first sentence.
- **A large first letter is a real drop capital.** On a page of running text, a large letter standing beside the first lines of a paragraph is written as a Word drop capital over those lines, so the paragraph wraps round it and it can be edited or removed with Word's Drop Cap command. On a page set freely over pictures it stays a text box of its own at its place.
- **A logo on every page is in the header.** In a Word file made from a PDF, a picture that stands at the same place at the top or bottom of at least three quarters of the pages is written once in the header or footer, so it can be changed in one place and stays out of the pages.
- **Pale and see-through text and colour from PDFs keep their transparency in Word.** A watermark such as DRAFT or a stamp such as PAID drawn at 10 or 30 percent printed solid over the text under it. It is now written with its transparency, and a see-through colour box is shaded with the colour it makes on the page. A box that is very faint, or lies on uneven colour, stays in the picture and the text over it stays ordinary text.
- **A contents page from a PDF is a Word table of contents.** In a Word file made from a PDF, a contents list whose entries are the titles of headings further on is written as Word's own table of contents, with each entry a link to its heading and the headings in the Navigation pane at the level the list shows; Update Table works on it. A PDF's own outline puts a bookmark on each heading it names, so a cross-reference can point there. A list with an entry that matches no heading stays plain text.
- **Footnotes from PDFs are Word footnotes.** A note at the foot of a page, with its raised mark in the text, was a paragraph at the foot of the page: when the page ran longer in Word the note moved to the next sheet or pushed text there. It is now a Word footnote with a footnote reference in the text, so Word keeps each note on the page of its reference, numbers it, and shows it in the Footnotes list. A symbol or a number that starts again on each page is kept as the note's own mark.

- **Captions are numbered by Word.** In a Word file made from a PDF, the number in a caption such as `Figure 2.` or `Table 1.` is a Word field when the numbers of that kind run 1, 2, 3 in order, so a figure or table added later renumbers the rest. Captions whose numbers skip stay text.
- **Word files made from tagged PDFs keep the document's own paragraphs, headings and tables.** A PDF saved from Word, Google Docs or LibreOffice carries tags that say which lines make a paragraph, which are headings and which are the rows and cells of a table. These are now read: lines are joined into paragraphs as the tags say, so a contents list or the lines of a code sample are no longer run together, headings get the level the author gave them, and a table without rules, or with only its cells shaded, is a Word table instead of shading and loose text. A table the grid finder read wrong where the tags put another one gives way to the tagged table. PDFs without tags are read as before.

### Changed
- **The PDF window forgets its size after a day.** It still opens where and how big you left it, maximised or not, but only if that was in the last 24 hours; after that it opens windowed at the default size in the middle of the screen. `pdfView.window` in `settings.json` gained `savedAt`, and a place saved without it counts as old.
- **A new Settings window.** Settings is one 880 x 600 window with a sidebar (General, Appearance, Engines, Updates, How Pomelo works, Diagnostics and About) and a search box that finds any setting or question. The Advanced button that made the page longer is gone and the window keeps its size. Appearance has a card for System, Light and Dark, and Engines shows how many engines are ready. The title bar is part of the page, with Windows drawing the minimise and close buttons in colours that follow the theme.
- **The guide is part of Settings.** The animation and the questions live under **How Pomelo works**, where each question is marked Q and its answer sits on its own panel. The separate guide window and the **?** button are gone; a new install opens Settings on that page.
- **A fuller tray menu.** It shows whether Pomelo is running or paused and has switches for Enabled and Start with Windows, then Settings, How Pomelo works (opens that page), Check for updates (opens Updates and checks) and Quit. The tray icon turns grey while Pomelo is paused, and the update toast now opens Settings on Updates.
- **Designed PDF pages keep their layout in Word.** A slide, a menu, a brochure, a résumé with a side panel or a cheat sheet of boxes was poured into the flow of the page and came out longer than the PDF, with its text moved and its pills and labels left behind. Such a page is now one picture of the page without its text, behind a text box for every paragraph at its place on the sheet, so every word is still real text you can select and edit, and the page cannot spill onto a second sheet. Text turned up, down or upside down, such as a side label, is a turned text box; text that is only pixels in a photo stays in the picture. Pages made of tables, such as invoices and statements, stay ordinary pages, and a scanned page keeps being read with OCR.

- **Equations from PDFs stay whole in Word.** A fraction, a root, a sum with limits or a matrix came apart in Word: the numerator, the bar and the denominator were separate lines that printed on top of one another, pushed the page onto the next sheet and left the equation number behind. Each such equation is now one picture cut from the page, set in its line of text with its number beside it, and its text is kept after it as hidden text, so search finds it. Equations written on a single baseline stay text, and an equation set in an ordinary text font is still read as lines. Equations are not rebuilt as Word equations: a half-right equation is worse than an exact picture.

- **Word files made from PDFs have real headers, footers and page numbers.** A running title or reference that repeats at the top or bottom of most pages is written once, as a Word header or footer, instead of as loose text on every page, and a page number is a page field ("1 of 8" is a page field and a page-count field), so it follows the pages when you edit. A page without them, such as a cover, has none. Pages set freely over pictures, scanned pages and pages stored sideways keep their text where it is.
- **Word files made from PDFs use Word's own styles.** Headings are Title and Heading 1 to 3, list items are List Paragraph and the caption of a figure or table is Caption, so the Navigation pane lists the headings and a new heading, list item or caption is made the same way. The body font and size are the Normal style, and the styles add no look of their own, so every page looks as it did. Where the PDF sets its lines at least as far apart as the font's own height, a line is "at least" that tall instead of exactly that tall, so lines make room when you make the text larger.
- **Columns of text in Word files made from PDFs are real Word columns.** Running text set in two or three columns was written as a table with a row for each paragraph, so Word read across the columns instead of down them and the text could not move from one column to the next. It is now one Word section with columns, the text of each column in turn; a title or a table above or below sits in a one-column section, and a picture, a table, a box, a centred caption or an equation with its number that stands in a column stays in it. Reading order against the PDF rose from 0.74 to 0.97 on the annual report, from 0.60 to 0.94 on the two-column paper and from 0.87 to 0.95 on the magazine. Columns that end at the same height are left to Word to balance, so text typed at the foot of one column runs on into the next; columns that end at different heights keep a column break, and text typed into one of them grows that column.

- **Tables from PDF files use Word table styles.** A table made from a PDF had a column on each side of every rule and a sliver of a column between a cell's fill and its text, no room between the text and the rules, and every border and fill written on each cell, so a column could not be resized or inserted, a row added at the end copied one cell's formatting, and Table Design showed no style. A table now has the PDF's own columns, the PDF's padding between rules and text, and a table style of its own (PDF Table 1, 2 and so on) that holds its rules, header row, stripes and first column. The header row repeats on the next sheet, an empty cell takes typed text, and any of Word's built-in designs can be picked in one click.
- **Tables on designed PDF pages are real Word tables.** On a slide, a menu, a datasheet or a cheat sheet, every cell of a table was a text box of its own, so a row could not be added and a column could not be resized. A ruled table, and three or more rows of text that line up in the same columns with no ruling, are now Word tables placed on the sheet where the PDF has them, and the page is still one sheet. The table draws its own rules and cell colours when nothing but the page's colour or a panel lies under it, so a row added later looks the same; otherwise the page keeps them and the table has no borders or shading. Rows are never an exact height. A row whose text runs into the next column stays a text box.
- **Word files made from PDFs read in the order of the PDF.** Select all, Tab and a screen reader went through a designed page's text boxes in a guessed order, and through a block of labels and values, or of columns, the wrong way round: all the labels and then all the values, or row by row where the PDF's author wrote column by column. The text boxes of a designed page now follow the order the PDF wrote the text in when that reads like a page, and lines side by side are rows when the PDF wrote them across and the cells of one row when it wrote them down each column. Reading order against the PDF rose from 0.88 to 1.00 on a brochure, 0.90 to 0.94 on slides, 0.92 to 0.96 on a menu, 0.95 to 0.98 on an invoice, 0.84 to 0.90 on a newsletter and 0.67 to 0.72 on a cheat sheet.
- **Stamps and tilted labels in Word files made from PDFs are text.** A stamp such as PAID, a tilted price badge or a slanted label was part of a picture, so its words could not be selected or edited and a word count missed them; only text turned by a quarter on a designed page was text. A line turned by any angle is now a text box turned by the same angle, on designed pages and on pages of running text, and a stamp's frame and colour stay in the picture.

- **Scanned pages keep their look in Word.** A scanned page came out as the recognised text alone, on white: the logo became the letters the OCR guessed, the signature and a stamp became large type, and the rules and the colour of the paper were lost. The scan now stays behind the text as a picture of the whole sheet, with the lines that are text painted out of it in the colour of the paper around them, so the logo, signature, stamp, rules and paper look as scanned while the letter stays ordinary editable paragraphs. A line more than 1.6 times the size of the body text, such as a large heading, stays in the picture unless it is a title on plain paper.
- **Text from PDFs grows with its font size in Word.** Where a PDF sets its lines closer together than the font's own height, Word kept each line at a fixed height, so making the text larger made the lines overlap. On a page of running text such a line is now a multiple of Word's own line height, so it grows with the text and looks the same as before. Lines in text boxes and in the rows of a table set on a designed page keep a fixed height.
- **Spacing in Word files made from PDFs is set the way Word sets it.** The empty one-point paragraph that held the gap above or below a table is gone: the gap is the space after the paragraph above or before the paragraph below, so Show All shows no empty paragraphs there. Gaps before paragraphs that differ only by rounding (a quarter point) are made one, and the most common gap is the Body Text style, so changing that style changes the spacing of all of its paragraphs.
- **Scanned pages are read in about half the time, with nothing extra to download.** OCR uses the recognition model that comes with the OCR package, which reads English as well as the English model that was downloaded during the install and is twice as fast. The install no longer downloads any model.

### Fixed
- **Shift does nothing on files it has no wheel for.** On a file that is not a PDF or a picture, tapping Shift no longer opens the conversion wheel, and Ctrl after it opens the wheel as usual instead of redrawing it. `onOpen` and `onSwitch` in `src/app/drop-flow.js` ignore a Shift with nothing to show and a switch to the wheel on show.
- **A wrong password on a 7z archive is always called wrong.** About one try in two hundred, 7-Zip answered a wrong password with only `Data Error : #0` and Pomelo showed that line instead of **Wrong password**. With a password given, a data error is now read as a wrong password.
- **Bullet lists from Google Docs stay lists in Word.** Google Docs puts a zero-width space after each bullet, so the bullets were not recognised and the items ran together as one paragraph with the next bullet at the end of the line. Zero-width spaces are dropped when the text is read, so each item is a list paragraph with one bullet and a gap.
- **Numbered clauses keep their numbers in Word.** The numbers of a contract set to the left of its text, such as clause 1 to 5 beside a column of comments, were placed outside the text column and did not print. The text column now starts at the number, so the number stands at the margin with the text indented after it. On a page set in text boxes each item counts from its own number, so clause 6 stays 6.
- **Numbered headings stay on one line in Word.** A heading such as `1.1  Key figures at a glance` was written as the number on one line and the title on the next. A bold section number and its title are one paragraph with a tab after the number.
- **Form fields from PDFs keep their boxes in Word.** On a form that is set as text, the check boxes, the radio buttons and the outlines of text areas and list boxes were missing, because a PDF keeps them in each field's appearance and not in the page. Check boxes and radio buttons are now characters in Segoe UI Symbol at their place, a box drawn as a square is a box character too, and an outline is a shape behind the text.
- **Small print in Word files made from PDFs keeps its letter spacing.** Text in a narrow font such as Arial Narrow was squeezed letter by letter, because it was measured in the PDF's own copy of the font, which can carry the metrics of another font. It is measured in the font Word uses.
- **Marks beside questions stay with their questions.** On a worksheet, the "[2 marks]" at the right of each question was moved after the last question, because the page was read as columns. Pieces that stand each on a row of their own are paragraphs in page order.
- **Maths from PDFs keeps its letters and symbols in Word.** A paper set in TeX names its maths fonts (CMMI, CMSY, MSAM and others), which a PC does not have, so Word chose a text font for them. Symbols and mathematical letters are now written in Cambria Math, and plain letters in an italic maths font are italic text.
- **Arabic and Hebrew from PDFs read from the right in Word.** A paragraph in Arabic or Hebrew was one left-aligned paragraph for each line, with the words of a mixed line in the wrong order and Arabic letters as the shapes the PDF stores. It is now one right-to-left paragraph aligned to its start, in reading order, with the base letters.
- **Scanned text keeps the space after a full stop.** The OCR often ran words together after a full stop or a colon ("Mr.Hassan", "Reference:DR/2026"). A space is put back when a lower-case letter, one of `. , ; ! ? :` and a capital word follow each other with no space. This applies to every format Pomelo makes from a scan.
- **Columns start level and the last line of a column is not stretched.** In a Word file made from a PDF, the column break sat inside the last paragraph of a column: Word stretched that paragraph's last line across the column and started the next column one line lower than the first. The break now has a paragraph of its own.
- **PDF to Word files print from Word.** A page whose content started left of the sheet gave the Word file a negative margin, and Word could not print it. No page margin is negative any more.
- **Justified text stays in paragraphs.** Justified lines that the PDF stores word by word came out as one paragraph per word, each indented further than the last. The words of such a line are joined again, the paragraph is justified, and a paragraph with an indented first line is no longer read as right-aligned. Rows of figures, tag pills and table cells stay apart.
- **Backgrounds stay on their page.** The background and the photos behind the text are anchored in the first paragraph of a page instead of its last one (or in its first table cell, when that cell has no fill and a table starts the page), so a page that runs over by a line no longer sends its background to the next sheet.
- **Pages stored sideways keep their size and stay editable.** A PDF page with a rotation, such as a form scanned sideways, is read upright and placed in a text box the size of the sheet, turned the way the PDF shows it. The text stays real text and the page keeps its size and orientation. Before, its text was read at the wrong coordinates and scattered over extra pages.
- **A photo at the foot of a page stays on its page.** A wide picture near the bottom, with its caption, is placed on the page instead of in the text, so a Word page whose text runs a little longer no longer pushes it onto a new sheet.
- **Photos cropped to a circle keep their shape.** The photo is kept as a real picture, cropped and shown as an ellipse, instead of a square picture plus a second copy of the same photo.
- **Cards with a soft shadow are no longer black.** A transparent shadow under a card was read as a solid black fill when the cards were found as a table.
- **Page footers and charts stay on their page.** Text in the bottom tenth of an upright page, well clear of the rest, is placed at its position on the sheet instead of after a long gap, and a picture that gridlines cross, or that text wraps right up against, is placed on the page instead of in the text. A Word page whose text runs a little longer no longer sends its footer to a sheet of its own or stacks a chart's labels under its picture.
- **Columns no longer wrap early in Word.** A paragraph in a table cell gave up 10 pt on its right that the cell did not have, and a line that filled its cell wrapped on any rounding. Cells keep no spare room now, a line that fills its cell is set 1 twip a character narrower, and a line that Word would set wider than the PDF is condensed by at most 0.8 pt a character.
- **Fonts missing from your PC are replaced by a close match.** A PDF font that is not installed is written as an installed serif, sans or mono family of about the same width (Cambria, Times New Roman, Georgia, Calibri, Arial, Segoe UI, Consolas or Courier New) and its lines are measured in that family, instead of leaving the choice to Word, which fell back to Times New Roman. The installed list comes from the registry and only font files inside the Windows fonts folders are read; a font file that cannot be opened is skipped.
- **Tables with an inset tint keep their figures as text.** A table whose cells are an outer colour with a lighter rectangle inset in each, as Word's table styles print, left thin strips between the two. The table finder dropped the whole grid because of them, and the figures of its two-line rows ended up in a picture. Cells narrower or lower than 8 pt are no longer cells, so the table is one real table.
- **Centred titles no longer start left of the page.** A centred paragraph on two lines was given a hanging indent from the different left edges of its lines, and Word cut off its first letters. A centred paragraph gets no first-line or hanging indent.
- **Letter-spaced titles stay on one line.** Word adds the letter spacing after the last letter too, so a large spaced title that filled its column broke inside the word and pushed the page down. The last letter of such a line is a run of its own without the spacing.
- **Outlined boxes keep their frames.** Two outlined boxes a few points apart were read as the rows of one table that kept only the top and bottom lines. They stay two boxes with four sides, and the edge of an outlined box is no longer also written as a separate rule.
- **A number that opens a paragraph is no longer a list item.** `(1) The Supplier ...` followed by lines set flush with the number was made a list with a hanging indent and its first line split off. It is one paragraph now, justified when the PDF's is, and a line that begins with a word starting in o (`of`, `on`, `or`, `one`) no longer loses that word to a bullet: only a lone `o` or a bullet glyph is a list marker.
- **Tall panels no longer lose text.** A table row more than half a sheet high may split across sheets; Word clipped such a row and the words in the clipped part were lost.
- **A hyphen that belongs to a word stays at a line end.** Where Word set a paragraph again, "three-year" lost its hyphen when the PDF broke the line there and came out as "threeyear". A word the text spells with a hyphen elsewhere keeps it, and so does a line that stops short of the right edge at a hyphen and a number such as forty-five ("fortyfive" before). A hyphen at the end of a justified line is still taken as the break of a word.
- **Typing at the end of a Word file made from a PDF shows.** The last paragraph of the document was one point high, so text typed after Ctrl+End came out in one-point type. It is an ordinary paragraph now.
- **Quotes and signatures are not headings.** A large pull quote, a long sentence, or a name under `Yours sincerely` was listed in Word's Navigation pane as a heading. Text of more than two lines or twelve words, with a quotation mark at either end, or a sentence ending in a full stop, stays a paragraph.
- **Paragraphs with a line break wrap where the PDF's do.** A paragraph that wraps by itself and also has a line break, such as an abstract above its keywords, was given the whole width of the page, so Word fitted a line fewer than the PDF and moved the columns below it up. It now keeps the width of the PDF's lines.
- **A flag in a table is not a list marker.** A first cell such as `-sU ` with a space after it was joined to the cell beside it as if it were a bullet, which split or shortened the table. Only bullet characters are bullets.
- **Table headers and stripes from PDFs keep their colour in Word.** Colour that a PDF draws as one rectangle across a row, a column or a whole table was dropped, so white header text printed white on white and striped rows came out plain. Such a fill now shades the cells under it. Colour that a Word table cannot paint, such as a bar across several days or an icon in a table, stays in a picture behind the table at its place instead of vanishing.
- **Text a PDF hides stays hidden in Word.** Text covered by a box, white on white, or in the PDF's invisible text mode was printed over the page or lost. It is now kept as Word hidden text: not shown, but found by search and shown by the Show/Hide button. Letters that let a picture show through them are not hidden: they stay in the picture.
- **A watermark across text no longer prints the text twice.** A large turned word, such as DRAFT, left every line it crossed in the picture of the word as well as in the document, so Word printed those lines on top of each other.
- **Signature lines and other rules from PDFs are kept in Word.** A short line to sign on, a "Date ____" line or a separator that was not a border or an underline was dropped. Such lines are now drawn where the PDF has them, with its colour, thickness and dash.
- **Rounded boxes keep their rounded corners in Word.** A box with rounded corners came out square. Its text is still ordinary editable text, and the shaded, outlined shape is drawn behind it. A rounded box inside a table cell stays square.
- **A coloured bar along the edge of a box is part of the box in Word.** The bar of a callout, on the edge or standing just beside it, was a separate picture that did not follow its box. It is now that side of the box's border.
- **Captions on photos keep their dark band in Word.** A band drawn over a photo was lost, so a white caption could print on the bare photo. The photo is now cut from the page as it looks, band included.
- **Scanned PDFs that already have searchable text keep it in Word.** A scan made by a scanner or an OCR program, a picture of the page with an invisible text layer, came out as the picture alone and the text was lost. The text is now real Word text over the picture, which is cut for it, the lines of a slightly skewed scan are written level, and a large title on plain paper is text too; logos, stamps and signatures stay in the picture, and a photograph turned by more than a few degrees stays a picture.
- **A drawing on a sideways page stays on one sheet in Word.** A site plan or diagram that a PDF stores turned by a quarter was written as flowing text, so the drawing and its labels ran onto a second sheet. It is now the page's picture, turned, with each label a turned text box, so it looks like the PDF and the labels are still text.
- **Only real headings are Word headings.** The day numbers of a calendar and the product names of a catalogue were listed as headings. A line in a table, without a letter, or in a look that a page repeats many times is no longer one.
- **Text set at double spacing is one paragraph.** Lines set far apart, as on ruled paper or a double-spaced page, were a paragraph each, so an edit did not re-wrap them. They now run on as one paragraph at the same line spacing.
- **Statements, letters and forms from PDFs are ordinary Word text, not text boxes.** A bank statement with shaded rows, a pleading on ruled paper, a thesis page of short lines and equations, an exam sheet and a one-page letter with a logo were each treated as a poster and written as hundreds of text boxes. A page now counts as designed only when pictures, colour or art really paint much of it, so these pages flow as paragraphs and tables that can be edited.
- **Every other row shaded still makes one table in Word.** A statement with a band behind every second row, and a month heading across a band, was written as a table for each shaded row with loose lines between them. The rows, headings and rules are now one table with the columns of the page, and its rows are as tall as the PDF's, so a long table no longer drifts down the sheet.
- **Marks in the margin of a statement no longer become a column.** The mailing and sorting bars printed at the edge of a statement were taken for a column of the page, so all of the text sat in a table beside them. They are now placed on the sheet as they are in the PDF.
- **An index or price list keeps each name and its number on one line in Word.** A name and its page number or price, set in columns, were written as two lines, so every entry took two. Each is now one line with a tab to the number, and the columns fit the sheet as they do in the PDF.
- **The labels of a chart are text in Word.** The numbers and words along the axes of a chart were left in its picture, so they could not be selected or searched. They are now text placed on the chart. A box that holds a sentence is not a chart.
- **A line of text beside a narrow column is no longer cut in Word.** A line placed outside the text column, such as a page number at the right edge of a pleading, was wrapped letter by letter at the margin. It now has a width of its own.
- **Contents pages and price lists keep their dot leaders and page numbers.** A line made of a title, a run of dots and a page number was written with the dots as text, so a line that Word set a little wider wrapped and pushed text onto extra sheets. It is now a tab with a dot leader, and a dotted or dashed line drawn between a name and its price is the leader of the tab between them (`split_leader`, `stroke_leaders`). A contents entry such as `1.1 Key figures ..... 3`, whose number is a piece of its own, has a right tab with the dot leader at the end of the line, so its page number no longer lands after the title, and the title is cut from the dots at the letters' own positions, so it is no longer condensed.
- **A page larger than Word allows is scaled to fit.** A PDF page longer than 22 inches on a side, such as a poster or a banner, came out at Word's largest size and was cut off. It is now scaled to fit with its proportions; its text stays text and its links stay links (`fit_sheets`).
- **Letter spacing is no longer made up for short words.** A word after a list marker or in a narrow cell, such as "Gym", came out with wide letter spacing and printed as "G y m". The spacing is now read from where the letters of the PDF start, in the font Word will draw them in, and is written only when the PDF has it.
- **A footer at the foot of the sheet or a rule below all the text no longer adds a sheet.** A title or page number set in the last 30 points of a page, or a rule below the text, was set after the text with a long space before it, so a page that came out a little taller in Word moved it onto an extra sheet. They are now placed on the sheet, and a frame that holds one line is as wide as its text.
- **A form's check boxes and footer stay on their page.** In a Word file made from a PDF, the check boxes of a form and the lines set on the sheet, such as a footer, were written after the text of their page, so a page the text filled sent them to a sheet of their own. They are now written before the text, and a line in the last 5 % of a sheet is placed on the sheet with them, so Word setting the page a little taller no longer pushes the last line over.
- **A bar or table that fills the sheet no longer leaves a blank sheet.** A slide with a coloured bar down one side, or a table whose last row reached the foot of the sheet, left no room for the paragraph Word needs after it and printed an empty sheet before the next page. A box, a row or a table now stops 4 pt short of the foot.
- **Very small text from PDFs is kept in Word.** Text under 2.5 pt, such as the labels on a diagram on a handout, was dropped. Anything a PDF shows down to 1 pt, the smallest size Word can set, is kept.
- **Spreadsheet pages stay Word tables.** A page of a workbook whose header shading ran on past the last column, or off the sheet, was not read as a table, so each cell became a text box of its own. A band along a row or column edge that covers the whole grid no longer counts as a box sticking out of it, a row shaded across several columns is a cell for each column, and a text that the PDF sets on one line with the next cell's is cut where that cell starts, so no cell is wider than its text and the rows keep their height. The data bars of a ledger stay behind the table at their place, and a rule that the PDF draws twice is one border, so neither adds a line to its row and a table the size of the sheet no longer spills onto the next.
- **Chevrons and other filled shapes in a diagram keep their place.** A filled shape that is not a rectangle, such as the chevrons of a process diagram, was dropped when it touched the outline of a box around or beside it. Only the faint ring a box leaves in the render is dropped now, and the shapes stay in the picture with their white labels as text.
- **The bars of a chart are no longer one big box.** Bars drawn as one path were written as a single shaded box over the whole chart. They stay in the picture of the chart.
- **A small chart on a page of text does not turn the page into a design page.** The numbers and labels of a climate chart or an infobox in an outlined panel made a page of running text a design page, with a text box for each line. They are now part of the panel.
- **Text that a PDF cuts off stays cut off.** A title wider than its cell, cut by the page's clip path, was written in full over the next cell. The part the PDF does not show is hidden text in Word: it is still in the file for search and copying, and it does not print. Letters that the edge of the sheet cuts are left as they are.
- **Text beside a picture or a box wraps round it in Word.** A paragraph that ran beside a thumbnail, a photo with its caption or a quote box was set in a narrow table cell, so Word's wider font broke every line again and left a staircase of one word to a line, with the picture in a column of its own. The picture or box is now anchored in the paragraph with square wrapping and the paragraph is one block of text across the column, as in Word, Google Docs and a browser print; a picture with its caption, or a box, is a text box that holds them and grows with its text.
- **Highlighted words stay highlighted.** A highlight drawn behind a few words of a line was left behind as a coloured band when the paragraph wrapped differently in Word. It is now shading on those words.
- **Drop capitals are found whatever the order.** A large first letter whose box reaches over the start of the lines beside it, as Word draws it, was left as a big letter under its paragraph. It is a Word drop capital over exactly the lines it stands beside.
- **Narrow justified columns stay one line.** The words of a justified line in a column that text wraps round, which the PDF holds one by one, came out as a paragraph for each word; they are joined into one line, and a word at the right edge of a narrow cell stands on a right tab, so Word cannot break it inside the word.
- **Underlines drawn as art no longer split a paragraph.** A small drawing laid over a line of text was written as a picture between two lines, which cut the paragraph in two and left a gap. It is placed on the page.
- **Justified papers keep their length in Word.** Word breaks lines without hyphenating, so text that a PDF sets with hyphens at its line ends came out a line longer in many paragraphs, and a two-column article ran onto extra sheets. When the PDF breaks words at the ends of its justified lines (a sample of the first 60 pages has at least 10 such breaks and at least one line in 67 ends so), Word's automatic hyphenation is switched on for justified paragraphs only; headings, captions and cells are set not to hyphenate.
- **A full last page no longer adds an empty sheet.** When the last page ends in columns that reach the foot of the sheet, the closing paragraph after them, a line of body height, went to a new sheet. It is now one point high on such a page, as on every other page; a last page without columns keeps an ordinary closing paragraph, so text typed there has the body size.
- **The colour scale of a figure stays with its figure.** A narrow picture against the panels of a figure, such as the colour scale of a spectrogram, was set in the line under the figure and moved the caption down by its height. A picture that touches a picture laid over others now keeps its place on the sheet too.
- **The tick labels of a plot stay at the plot.** The numbers at the side and under a plot drawn as curves were separate lines of text, each in its own paragraph, and left a gap in the column beside them. Three or more short labels one under another within 30 points of a drawing that holds a label of its own are positioned with it, as the labels inside the drawing already were.
- **A plot is not read as a table.** The frame and gridlines of a plot, with its curves across them, were found as a grid and written as a table of the axis numbers. A grid that a path of ten or more curved or slanted segments fills a fifth of is left a drawing.
- **The column of labels of a ruled table is part of the table.** In a table set with rules over and under all its columns but vertical rules only to the right of the first, as LaTeX writes `l|cc`, the labels in the first column were left outside as a paragraph with line breaks. The column that the long rules reach is now a column of the table.

- **A photographed page stays a photograph in Word.** A page photographed with a phone, curled, lit unevenly or slanted, had its lines painted out of the picture one by one and typed level over it, so half a line was picture and half type, a receipt's amounts landed on the wrong rows and a hand-written note was replaced by what the OCR guessed. Such a page, whether its lines are slanted by 2 degrees or more, a quarter of them more than a degree off the rest, or the paper's light differs by more than a tenth across the sheet, now keeps the whole photo as the page and its text as hidden text, so search still finds it.
- **A stamp on a scan stays as it was stamped.** A stamp or a slanted label on a scanned page was half painted out and its words typed level over what was left, and a line that a stamp crossed was painted out with it. A line that a stamp's colour lies over, or that is slanted against the other lines, now stays in the picture and its words are hidden text.
- **Writing in coloured pen on a scan stays as written.** Lines written by hand in blue, green or red ink over a scanned page were typed out in a plain font as if they were print, and read wrongly where the hand was loose. A line with colour in its ink now stays in the picture and its words are hidden text. Coloured print on a scan stays in the picture too, and handwriting in black or grey ink is still typed like print.
- **The back of a scanned sheet no longer turns into text.** A blank page whose other side showed through was read by OCR and the reflection came out as lines of nonsense. Text whose ink is less than 40 grey levels darker than the paper is not written.
- **A sideways scan prints its text once.** A scanned page stored sideways, with a text layer that turns with it, printed the layer over the page's own picture of the same words. The layer is now hidden text and the picture is the page.
- **The text of a scanner's layer is set in one size.** A scanner sizes every word of its text layer by its own box, so a line printed in a mix of sizes and pieces of one line landed on rows of their own. Each line is now set in the size most of its letters have, and the pieces of a row are joined again.
- **Photos that a PDF turns over show the right way up.** A picture that a page draws mirrored or upside down, such as the photo on a page stored turned by 180 degrees, was copied from the file as it is stored and printed upside down in Word. It is now cut from the page as the page shows it.
- **A centred title no longer breaks a word.** A title of several centred lines that sets the margins of its page had no room beyond its widest line, so a font a little wider than the PDF's split a word of it over two lines. The paragraph reaches past the margins by a little on both sides and stays centred.
- **A stamp is not printed twice.** The word of a stamp added with a PDF reader's stamp tool, such as CONFIDENTIAL or DRAFT, was written as text over the picture of the stamp, which already shows it, so it looked bold or doubled. It is kept as hidden text, so search still finds it.
- **Code keeps its indents.** In a block of code with a line that runs to the right edge, the indented line after it was joined to it and lost its indent. A line that starts with blanks is never the continuation of the line before.
- **A note under a table stays under it.** A line just below a table, such as a list of references, was cut up as if it were a row of the table and printed on top of the last row. Text covered by a rounded box that the PDF draws over it, such as a practice note over a table, is hidden as it is under any other box.
- **Notes that run past a spreadsheet's last column stay on their sheet.** A note that spills out of the last column into the empty cells beside it was a paragraph after the table, so a full sheet grew a sheet of its own. It is placed on the sheet at its row.
- **Ruled lines for notes print.** Several ruled lines one under another, as on a handout or a worksheet, printed as one line: Word draws neighbouring paragraphs with the same border and indents as a single block. Each line now differs by a twip from the one before.
- **PDFs with a damaged text map convert to Word.** A PDF whose font maps a letter to half of a UTF-16 surrogate pair stopped the conversion with an XML error, so no Word file was made. Such a character is now dropped with the other characters an XML file cannot hold (`BAD_XML` in `pdf_spans.py`); the rest of the line is kept.
- **Scanned pages keep their length in Word.** The words read from a scan were joined by non-breaking spaces, because the font they are written in gives its space to that character as well, so Word could not wrap a line that came out a little wider and broke it inside a word, pushing the rest of the page onto an extra sheet. The words of a scanned page are now parted by ordinary spaces.
- **Light text on a dark ground in a scan keeps its look.** A screenshot of a terminal or a slide's coloured band in a scanned page had its light letters painted out and typed again in black over the dark ground. Such a line now stays in the picture, and its words are hidden text so search still finds them (`on_dark` in `convert.py`).
- **Numbered items whose lines wrap back under the number stay apart.** In a list typed in Word without a hanging indent, the number of the next item ran on at the end of the item before ("T / F 5."). A line that starts with a number no longer joins a paragraph that already holds a numbered line.
- **Thin borders of a box no longer push a page down.** The half-point side borders of a box, which Word prints as thin filled rectangles, could be cut out as two narrow pictures, each on a line of its own, so the page ran a sheet long. They are taken as borders, which the box already draws.
- **A frame drawn as one outline is no longer a black box.** A frame whose border is one filled path traced round both its edges was taken for a filled box, so the table inside it was shaded black.
- **A justified line held in two pieces stays one line.** A line of a justified paragraph that the PDF stores as two pieces a wide gap apart was read as text and a right tab, so its last words stood apart and the line broke there in Word.
- **Items after an "o" bullet run on to their next line.** When the text after a bullet started with a space, its second line did not join it and stood as a paragraph of its own.
- **A turned column head that the print area hides is hidden in Word.** A spreadsheet printed in several pages repeats a turned column head under the first column of the next page, where the PDF hides it; it printed over the head beside it. It is now hidden text.
- **Boxes in a row stay in a row.** The three boxes under a chart of connectors, as in an organisation chart, printed one under another and one step further along each, because the connectors were counted as part of the row. The row is a row of cells again.

### Internal
- **Window code shared.** The title row, tabs, panels and cards of the PDF window now live in src/ui-shared so another window can use them. `createAppWindow` in `src/shared/electron/windows.js` makes the window, `startBounds` is in `src/shared/core/bounds.js`, and the card frame is `src/ui-shared/card.js`.
- **Steadier window and helper tests.** The page grid tests scroll each page into view, probe the first row and run at the smallest window, and one text-selection test presses above the wider dock. The helper stop test waits for a helper that is running, instead of aborting a three-page edit after 30 ms, which could finish before its process was taken down on a busy runner.
- **An edit helper for the PDF window.** `python/pdf_edit.py <in.pdf> <out.pdf> <ops.json>` applies a checked list of changes (`pages.rotate`, `pages.delete`) to a PDF in a session folder and writes the next version; `pdf_info.py properties` reads the facts of a file as JSON. The list is checked in `ops.js` before anything is written and again by the helper, so only the keys an operation takes get through. A change that edits in place is appended to the file, anything that has to drop content writes it whole, and a protected file stays protected. The password reaches the helper on standard input, one line of JSON, and is replaced by `***` in any reason it prints. `conversion.runPdfTool` runs the helpers, and `pdfws:apply` makes the next version of a document from a list; no control in the window uses it.
- **The PDF window keeps changes as versions and saves them.** A change handed over by the page becomes a revision in a session folder (`revisions.js`, `documents.js`, at most 30, within a disk budget, `pdfws:put-revision`, `pdfws:undo`, `pdfws:redo`), and **Save** (Ctrl+S) replaces the file with `replaceFile` in `src/shared/node/fs.js`: .NET's `File.Replace` through PowerShell, which keeps permissions, creation time and the downloaded-file mark and works while Pomelo holds the file open, and stops with `changed`, `read-only`, `no-space` or `locked` and the file untouched. **Save a copy** (Ctrl+Shift+S) writes to a new name. A document with changes shows a dot after its name and a Save button; closing a tab, closing the window and quitting ask `Save changes to <name>?`. Without the PDF engine pdf.js's own save is taken up to 100 MB (`NO_ENGINE_SAVE_LIMIT`). No tool in the window makes a change; the page's `workspaceApi.putRevision` is the way in. `runStatus` takes an `env` option and the conversion feature exports `uniquePath`.
- **One viewer for all the PDF window's tabs.** `ui/viewer.js` makes one `PDFViewer` and sets each tab's document into it with `setDocument`; every module that holds something of a document clears it on `pagesdestroy` and builds it again on `pagesinit`. `documents.js` gains `find`, `list` and `close`, and `pdfws:close`, `pdfws:drop` and `pdfws:opened` carry the tabs. The new settings key `pdfRecent` holds up to ten paths, newest first. The right-click entry is `SystemFileAssociations\.pdf\shell\PomeloOpen`, removed with the others, and `--open` starts that come within 400 ms are opened together.
- **7-Zip no longer waits at a password prompt.** `runCapture` closes the program's standard input when it has none to give (and writes `input` when it has), so an encrypted archive fails at once instead of waiting for an hour. `engine-kit/seven-zip.js` runs 7-Zip with a password on stdin and turns its two ways of saying "protected" into a `PasswordError`.
- **Passwords from a page.** `#shared/node/secrets.js` checks a password (a one-line string of 1 to 4096 characters) and holds it as a Buffer; the PDF window uses it too.
- **The input helper takes several trigger keys.** `keys <name> <name>…` in `native/pomelo-input.cs` sets the keys it reports (`ctrl`, `alt` or `shift`; a bad name makes the whole command ignored), next to `key`, which still sets one, and the default is still Ctrl. The ready line is now `{"ready":true,"keys":true}`; an older helper sends `{"ready":true}`, so `InputHelper.keysSupported` tells the app whether Shift can arrive and the conversion wheel names it only then. `Trigger` emits `open` with `{ wheel }` and `switch`, and `createDropFlow` takes `openPdfs` and `keysSupported`. New unit suites `trigger` and `drop-flow`; `pipeline` and `overlay-shot` cover both wheels, the turn and reduced motion.
- **`blockNetwork(session)` and the exact `pdfjs-dist` version.** The request filter of the print window moved from `lib/print-pdf.js` into `src/shared/electron/windows.js`, so the PDF workspace's window uses the same one. `pdfjs-dist` is pinned to `6.5.438` without a caret, because the transport and the viewer it is used through are internal to that version.
- **`pdfView` in `settings.json`.** Holds the PDF workspace's window place as `{ window: { x, y, width, height, maximised } }`, written by the main process only; `settings:set` still drops everything it does not list.
- **Settings shares its look.** The surface colours, the reset, the buttons, the title bar and the line icons moved out of `settings.css` and `settings.js` into `src/ui-shared/` (`tokens.css`, `controls.css`, `icons.js`), where any window can load them instead of copying CSS. The Settings screenshots are unchanged in both themes.
- **Folders that outlive a function call.** `makeTempDir(tag)` in `src/shared/node/fs.js` makes a temp folder that stays until the caller removes it, named like the other temp paths with the tag at the end, and `sweepTempDirs(tag, maxAgeMs)` removes the folders of that tag that were last changed longer ago than `maxAgeMs`, so a run that crashed leaves nothing behind for long. `withTempDir` is unchanged.
- **The fidelity check reads footnotes.** `test/fidelity/compare.py` counts the words of `footnotes.xml` and `endnotes.xml` with those of the body, so a note that moved out of the body is not scored as lost text.
- **The check for text never written counts joined lines.** With `POMELO_DEBUG=1`, the list of lines that were read and not written leaves out a line that was joined into another, such as a contents entry and its page number, so a line on that list is a real loss. The layout suite fails when any drawn PDF prints one, and its time limit is 420 s.
- **The order check leaves running titles out.** `test/fidelity/compare.py` takes the lines in the top or bottom 8 % of a page that come back on two or more pages out of the order score on both sides (recall still counts them), because Word prints a header and a footer before the page's text wherever the source draws them. It also reads Arabic presentation forms as the letters behind them.
- **The fidelity check joins words broken at a line end.** `plain_words` in `test/fidelity/compare.py` joins a word that ends a line with a hyphen to the lowercase word that starts the next one, in the source and in Word's printout alike. Word hyphenates in other places than a TeX or word-processor source, so the halves of a hyphenated word counted as lost words whatever the converter wrote.
- **Pages are classified.** Each page the converter handles is a design page (pictures or colour over more than 40 % of the sheet, a coloured background, a colour panel holding text, two or more boxes holding text of their own, or many short pieces of text among pictures or boxes outside a table) or a document page; a page with no text is always a document page. What is painted counts, not the boxes round it (a grid of rules or the shaded rows of a table are not a design), and short pieces of text count by their words. `POMELO_DEBUG=1` prints the class of each page and why. A design page is written as in the entry above; a document page is written as before.
- **Headers, footers and styles.** `pdf_flow/structure.py` finds the lines in the top or bottom tenth of the sheet whose text, with page numbers read as `PAGE` and `NUMPAGES`, repeats on at least three quarters of the pages (`Furniture`), takes them out of the flow of a document page, and writes them as framed paragraphs in header and footer parts. A section carries references only where its header or footer differs from the section before it, so a page without one (a cover) gets an empty part. A paragraph's line spacing is `atLeast` when the PDF's pitch is at least the height Word gives the font (read from the font's `hhea` table by `line_height` in `pdf_fonts.py`) or 1.32 times the size, and a multiple of the font's height (`auto`) where the pitch is tighter than the font, sizes are mixed, the text is not Latin or the font is not installed (`exact` inside a text box or a row of a placed table). `set_styles` writes Normal (the body font and size), Title, Heading 1 to 3, List Paragraph and Caption with no look of their own, and a run states its font and size only where they differ from Normal. `compare.py --docx-words` counts the words of the header and footer parts too.
- **Columns and sections.** `column_runs` in `pdf_flow/builder.py` finds runs of consecutive bands whose lines (and inline pictures) sit in two or three columns with the same gutter, splits a run at a band that does not belong (a line across a gutter, a contents line with its page number) and keeps what is left only when each column has at least five lines, the columns are about as wide as each other, the gutter is narrow and the lines fill their columns. `columns` writes the run as a continuous section; the section's properties are written by `end_sections` in `convert.py` once the page's margins are known, which is why a page now has more than one section and `convert_page` returns the header and footer of each. `compare.py` counts a header or footer once for every section that starts on a new sheet.
- **Unique drawing ids and a guard for headers and footers.** Every picture and text box in a Word file made from a PDF takes its id from one counter for the whole document (`Lists.drawing_ids`), where each page used to have a range of its own: a page with more than 200 drawings, or a document of about 500 pages, reused ids, and Word may report such a file as unreadable and repair it. `convert` also checks that the sections a page adds match the header and footer states it returns, and `attach` keeps the header and footer text in the first section instead of dropping it when the counts differ. `test/fidelity/run.js` no longer fails when a run without `--word` cannot render a file.
- **Check PDF to Word with Microsoft Word.** `node test/fidelity/run.js --word` prints each converted DOCX with Microsoft Word and reports pages, recall, order and visual similarity against the original, with a 120 s limit per file and a Word of its own for every file. `compare.py --docx-words` counts how many source words the DOCX holds, treating tabs as spaces, and runs without Word. `--out` moves the results out of `test/fidelity/out`.
- **Editability in the Word check.** `node test/fidelity/run.js --word` also prints, for each converted file, the share of the document's words inside text boxes (`tb`), the number of Word tables, the number of heading paragraphs and the number of paragraphs with text whose line height is fixed (`exact`) and the number of words written as hidden text (`hidden`, which are left out of the recall and order of the printout, because Word does not print them; the words are listed in `report.json`). `compare.py --docx-words` returns the same figures. A page scaled to fit Word is compared at its new size, and a word made only of punctuation, such as a leader dot, is not counted.
- **Settings accepts only known values.** `settings:set` keeps `enabled`, `openAtLogin` and `autoUpdate` (booleans), `theme` (`system`, `light` or `dark`) and `outputDir` only as `''`; anything else in a patch, including the update checker's keys, is dropped.
- **Packaging ships the changelog.** `tools/package.js` no longer excludes `CHANGELOG.md`.
- **Score PDF to Word on tagged PDFs.** `test/fidelity/tagged.py` converts each tagged PDF of a folder as it is and with its tags taken out, and reports the share of tagged paragraphs, headings and tables the Word file has, so any folder of PDFs saved from an office program scores the converter on files it was never tuned on.
- **No OCR warm-up step.** `python/ocr_warmup.py` and the install step that ran it are gone: they only downloaded the English model that OCR no longer uses.

The drag gesture works exactly as before. On Windows 11 the entry is under **Show more options**. Pomelo adds it for your user account the first time it starts and removes it when you uninstall.
- **A guide for new users.** The first time Pomelo starts it opens a short guide with an animation of drag, tap Ctrl and drop, and a few questions and answers, with step-by-step instructions and pictures of where Pomelo sits in the tray and where the right-click entry is. The new **?** button next to the name in Settings opens it again; the animation shows a document, a spreadsheet and a picture being converted in turn.
- **Save conversions to a folder you choose.** Settings has a new **Save conversions to** dropdown. Leave it on **Default** and files are saved next to the original, as before; choose a folder and every conversion lands there, all in one place. If the folder you picked is gone, Pomelo says so and saves next to the original instead. Your original files are never changed.
- **Light and dark themes.** Settings has an Appearance control with System, Light and Dark. System follows Windows, as before; Light and Dark apply to every Pomelo window.
- **Source code and licences.** Advanced settings has a link to the source code and licences, and the third-party notices say where the source of FFmpeg and 7-Zip can be downloaded.

### Changed
- **The wheel's centre is now a Pomelo file.** While you hold a file over the wheel, its centre shows the file as a coloured icon with its format on it and a picture of its own: every format Pomelo reads (DOCX, XLSX, PNG, JPG, MP4, MP3, ZIP and the rest) has a different one, and a folder shows as a folder. Dragging several files shows a small stack with a count. Pointing at a format on the ring adds "to PDF" under the icon.
- **The trigger key is always Ctrl.** The Trigger key setting is gone from Settings, because Pomelo will use other keys for other gestures later. Drag a file and tap Ctrl, as before.
- **A cleaner update.** With **Check automatically** on, the toast now says "Update your Pomelo to v...", and clicking it opens Settings with the **Download update** button showing. Pressing the button opens a small **Pomelo Downloader** window with the stage, the percentage, the size and time left, and a Cancel button; you can minimise it. The installer's own window no longer appears. When the update is in, Pomelo reopens by itself (with Settings open again if you had it open) and a toast says it is now on the new version.
- **New engine parts install with an update.** An update that adds an engine to the PDF components you already have (as 0.1.3 did with OCR) now adds it for you, in the same window, instead of leaving you to press Install in Settings.

### Fixed
- **The wheel always opens on top.** On some PCs the format wheel opened behind the folder you were dragging from, so only the part outside that window showed.
- **No command window during an update.** A command window flashed on screen for a moment while Pomelo updated itself. The installer now waits for Pomelo to close on its own instead of using the pause that opened it.

## [0.1.4] - 05-11-2026

### Fixed
- **PDF to PowerPoint keeps text editable.** Text was left in the slide's background picture, where it cannot be edited, whenever it held a curly quote, a dash or a CJK character. It is now real text on the slide. Only Hebrew, Arabic and other right-to-left text, which cannot be drawn correctly as editable text, stays in the picture.

## [0.1.3] - 04-11-2026

### Changed
- **PDF to Word is always editable.** The new converter rebuilds every page as real paragraphs (indents, alignment, line spacing, fonts, colours, underline, highlight), real bullet and numbered lists, real Word tables (ruled tables, tables drawn with shaded rows, and side-by-side blocks such as columns as borderless tables), shaded and outlined boxes, hyperlinks, a stretched colour for a full-page background, and pictures. Pictures, charts, logos and gradients stay pictures; text on a picture is a positioned paragraph you can still retype. The old "exact" mode, which wrote every line as its own frame, and the **PDF to Word > Layout** switch in Settings are gone.
- **Scanned PDFs are read with OCR.** RapidOCR runs offline, with models fetched once at install. A page that is only a picture is turned into text first, so PDF to Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Markdown, HTML, text and EPUB all work on scans.
- **A smaller PDF engine.** It is now PyMuPDF (pinned to 1.28.2), python-docx, RapidOCR and pillow-heif. A fresh install used to pull in PyMuPDF 2.x with its layout-analysis stack and `pdf2docx` (435 MB on disk); it is now 360 MB including OCR. **Settings > Advanced > Conversion engines** has a new **OCR** row; an existing PDF engine is rebuilt when you press Install.
- **A clearer engine page in the installer.** It says what the PDF choice contains (PDF to Word and more, OCR, HEIC; about 320 MB).
- **Failures report the best reason.** A conversion that fails reports the reason from the best engine instead of the last fallback's.

### Fixed
- **PDF to Word is much faster on text pages.** Every page that has nothing but text took 5 to 12 seconds (a per-pixel blank-page check in Python): 30 such pages took 161 seconds and a 110-page document hit the ten-minute limit. 40 pages now convert in about 6 seconds, and the time limit grows with the size of the PDF.
- **Scanned PDFs give real text.** A scanned PDF "converted" to a Word file with no text in it. It is now read with OCR, or says plainly that it needs the OCR component.
- **Plain reasons for failures.** A password-protected PDF and a damaged file each say so, without file paths or Python errors.
- **Fewer lost letters in PDF to Word.** Rotated text over other text lost letters ("PAID" became "PAI"), letter-spaced capitals came out with spaces inside the words ("I NVO I C E"), and white text on a shaded header disappeared.

### Internal
- **The installer is tested for real in CI.** The installer test installs, updates and uninstalls the real installer.

## [0.1.2] - 03-11-2026

### Changed
- **Update now installs the update for you.** Updating no longer sends you to the browser to run the installer again. Pomelo downloads the new version itself (with progress and time left), checks it against the SHA-256 published with the release, installs it quietly over the current one and starts again. It only downloads from GitHub, follows redirects only to GitHub's own hosts, throws away a download that is incomplete or does not match, and falls back to the download page when a release has no checksum. Settings, shortcuts and engines are left as they are.

### Fixed
- **The new version replaces the old one.** Installing over an older Pomelo could leave the old version running: a copy started from somewhere else (or not called `Pomelo.exe`) kept Pomelo's "only one copy" lock, so opening the new one just showed the old one's window and its old version number. The installer now stops whichever process holds that lock, and stops with a clear message if it cannot.
- **The installer says when Pomelo is already installed.** It shows which version, where, and that it will be updated (or reinstalled). An update goes into the folder Pomelo is already in and no longer asks about the folder or the conversion engines again; settings, shortcuts and engines are kept.

## [0.1.1] - 03-11-2026

### Added
- **Automatic update checks.** **Check automatically** in Settings (off by default). When on, Pomelo looks for a newer version 30 seconds after it starts and then once a day, and says so in a toast and on the tray icon when one exists. It never downloads or installs anything: the download starts only when you press **Download update**. A failed check is silent and retried a couple of hours later.
- **A PDF to Word layout choice.** **PDF to Word > Layout** in Settings > Advanced: **Exact** (the default) or **Editable**.

### Changed
- **PDF to Word is exact by default.** Every page keeps its size, positions, fonts, sizes, colours, spacing, links, pictures, shapes, rules and backgrounds. Text stays editable in place; whatever is not text (pictures, shapes, gradients, rules) sits behind it as the page background, and text that cannot be re-typed faithfully (right-to-left, rotated, symbol-font glyphs) stays in that background so it still looks right. A new **PDF to Word > Layout** setting (Advanced) switches to **Editable**, which rebuilds flowing paragraphs; designed pages (shapes or pictures behind text, columns, rotated text) are still converted exactly.
- **Progress on every toast.** The ring fills as a conversion goes (the cancel X stays in its middle), and the text beside it gives the percentage and about how long is left. Engines that can measure their progress report it (video and audio, pages of a PDF, archives); for the rest (LibreOffice and friends) the figure is an estimate that follows the clock, learned from how long that kind of conversion has taken on this PC before. The ring no longer just spins.
- **A better PDF reader for text formats.** PDF to Markdown, HTML, text and EPUB are read by one new reader (PyMuPDF): all text in reading order, columns in order, headings, lists, tables, links, bold and italic, code and pictures. The old Markdown route (pymupdf4llm) dropped text (a résumé lost 5%, an invoice a third) and is gone, along with its pinned install.
- **PDF to Excel works without ruled tables.** It finds borderless grids, and otherwise gives each page's text lines in a sheet.
- **PDF to PowerPoint keeps positions and links.** Text boxes sit at the original positions on a picture of everything else, and links are kept (they were lost before). PDF to RTF uses the flowing rebuild, since LibreOffice folded every page of an exact layout into one.
- **Better DOCX to PDF without LibreOffice.** The built-in conversion takes the page size, margins, default font and size from the document and no longer prints to the very edge of the sheet; Markdown, text and HTML copies get proper page margins too.
- **A smaller PDF engine download.** The optional PDF engine no longer installs `pymupdf4llm`, so it is smaller to download and carries one less licence to track.

### Fixed
- **Editable PDF to Word opens cleanly.** Hyperlinks were written inside text runs, which the file format forbids, so link text vanished in LibreOffice and could trip Word. Bullet lists and skill lines were turned into fixed-height two-column tables that clipped text (about 7% of the words in a test résumé); lone spaces between styled words were dropped (Stack:PHP); bullets are now real Word lists.

### Internal
- **A fidelity harness.** `npm run fidelity:samples` then `npm run test:fidelity` convert PDFs, print the result back to PDF and score text, positions, fonts, colours, links and look against the original.

## [0.1.0] - 03-11-2026

### Added
- **Drag, tap Ctrl, drop.** Drag a file, folder or desktop icon, tap Ctrl, and drop it on a format wheel to convert it next to the original.
- **Many formats.** Conversions for images (incl. HEIC), video, audio, PDF, documents, EPUB, slides, spreadsheets, subtitles, archives (ZIP, 7Z, TAR, GZIP, RAR input) and folders.
- **High-fidelity PDF conversion.** PDF to Word, Excel and Markdown through optional Python engines; LibreOffice and Pandoc are used when installed.
- **Parallel conversions.** A toast stack in the bottom-right corner folds into one progress ring beyond three.
- **A Settings window.** An everyday view and an Advanced view, with Check for updates.
- **Cancel any conversion.** A cancel button (✕ in the middle of the progress ring) on every running conversion and on the summary toast; helper programs are stopped and partial files deleted.
- **One-click engine installs.** **Install** for optional engines (PDF fidelity, HEIC export, LibreOffice, Pandoc) in Settings > Advanced, and an **Uninstall** button that removes Pomelo and only the engines Pomelo installed.
- **Start with Windows.** On by default.
- **A Windows installer.** Inno Setup, per-user, no administrator rights, three screens (location, optional conversion engines, shortcuts). Silent install: `/SILENT`, optionally `/ENGINES=pdf2docx,soffice,pandoc`.

### Changed
- **LibreOffice installs without administrator rights.** It is no longer installed with winget. Its official MSI is downloaded (hash-checked) and unpacked into Pomelo's own folder, so there is no administrator prompt and no desktop shortcut, and uninstalling is deleting the folder.
- **Live progress in the installer.** It shows which engine is installing, how far and how long, and its finish page says which one to retry if an engine fails.
- **Uninstalling from Pomelo shows the Windows uninstaller.** It opens with its progress window, instead of a hidden command-line step.
- **A desktop shortcut by default.** The desktop shortcut option is on by default.
- **A native drag helper.** Drag detection now runs in a small native helper (`pomelo-input.exe`) instead of a PowerShell script. It reports the trigger key and Esc only while the left mouse button is held, and no longer writes into Explorer's memory to find desktop icons.
- **A new logo and icon set.** Flat and minimal.
- **Renamed to Pomelo.** With a pomelo-pink and rind-green look and a new logo.
- **Better GIF export.** GIF export keeps the whole clip (frame rate and size scale down for longer clips, 5 minute limit) and animated GIF/WebP keep their animation when converted to each other.

### Internal
- **Engine commands.** Headless `Pomelo.exe --install-engines=...` and `--uninstall-engines`, used by the installer and uninstaller.
- **A release workflow.** It builds the installer from a version tag into a draft GitHub release, with a SHA-256 checksum and a build attestation.
